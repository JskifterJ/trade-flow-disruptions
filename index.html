How to read this map (2-minute primer)
  1. Producers are far from consumers. The Gulf, Russia, the US, West Africa, Brazil, Qatar and Australia export; China, India, Japan, Korea and Europe import. Almost all of it goes by sea.
  2. Ships funnel through a handful of chokepoints (yellow diamonds). Click one to see what passes through it and roughly how much. Hormuz and Malacca alone carry about 40% of seaborne oil.
  3. Prices connect the routes. A cargo goes where the price difference beats the freight cost (the "arbitrage"). When a chokepoint closes, ships take longer routes, freight jumps, and the regions that relied on that flow bid up the benchmark they buy on.

Line width and labels show each route's sourced 2025 volume (mb/d for oil, Mt for LNG) and its share of that commodity's seaborne trade, so a thick crude line and a thick LNG line both mean a big slice of their market.
